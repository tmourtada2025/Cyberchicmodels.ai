## Purpose

This defines the roster CyberChic builds to — not what it happens to own. It is a casting board engineered from 2026 market demand, weighted toward the underserved segments that form the competitive moat. Every slot is a build target. The existing trained models are mapped against this board in a separate step; this document assumes zero inventory.

---

## The one rule every model must clear

**Would a brand license this face because they can't easily get it elsewhere?**

If yes, it earns a slot. If it is another conventionally pretty AI face, it does not. Self-serve tools already generate generic pretty faces for a few dollars — that lane is commoditized to near-zero. The moat is **distinctiveness and persistent, licensable identity**, not price. Price is the hook; distinctiveness is the business.

Corollary: **distinctive-over-pretty.** Per 2026 casting directors, "perfection feels less interesting than it used to — the faces that stay with me are the ones that leave a question mark." Build the question marks.

---

## Standing constraints

- **Female only** for this roster. Male is a later phase. Non-binary is excluded.
- **Brand-neutral positioning.** No Pride/LGBTQ or activist theming in the catalogue or on the site. Demographic range is driven by commercial demand, never messaging.
- **Age floor — hard rule:** every model must read as clearly adult, **25 or older**. No youthful, teen, or age-ambiguous reads. This is a brand-safety and platform-compliance requirement, not a style choice.
- **Register discipline:** editorial/commercial leads every model's public book. Glamour/boudoir is walled off, never the front.
- **Ethnicity is tagged at high resolution** — Peruvian, not "Latina"; Japanese, not "Asian"; Nigerian, not "African." The specificity is the product and the searchability. A face tagged only by region is undervalued by the buyer.

## Roster size and balance

**Target: 45 female models**, phased. Upper-mid depth — enough to read as an established agency, tight enough that every face is distinctive.

**Balanced across two registers** (not commercial-primary, not luxury-primary):

| Register | Target | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial / lifestyle / e-commerce / beauty | ~24 | Volume, bookable, everyday revenue |
| Luxury / editorial / events / **bridal** | ~21 | Margin, prestige, high per-booking value |

Note: **bridal and high-end events sit in the LUXURY tier, not commercial** — Asian, Gulf, and Indian communities spend heavily on weddings and events, and the category is under-cast. It is one of the strongest revenue slots.

## Every model ships 5 looks

Per 2026 casting data: **models with 5 versatile looks book 4× more often than models with one look.** Versatility is the booking multiplier — and it is the one thing AI models do structurally better than humans (one identity, many registers, on demand). Every model's book carries five distinct looks spanning her register and specialty.

## Build-priority vs. display-priority — two separate levers

- **Build-priority (production):** weight generation effort toward the gaps — distinctive, mature, plus-size, natural-Afro, untapped-region. That is where the moat is made.
- **Display-priority (homepage):** lead the front page with the distinctive faces — **because distinctiveness converts**, not as a diversity statement. The visitor should think "these are stunning, rare models," never "this is a diversity checkbox." Feature them as the best and rarest product, brand-neutral.

Both levers point the same way here, for the same reason: the underserved faces are the moat.

---

## Positioning it sells against

- **Cheap self-serve AI tools (~$3/face):** they flood generic-pretty. You do not compete there. You sell the faces they cannot make and the persistent identity they cannot hold.
- **Human agencies (~$1,200/day + rights):** you are a fraction of the cost (industry benchmark ≈ 95% lower, ~$7.50 vs ~$205 per delivered image), and you field the range they structurally underserve — mature, plus-size, natural-Afro, untapped-region.
- **Luxury runway:** still Eurocentric and tokenistic (only ~11% racialized leads in top-house campaigns). Aim at commercial, lifestyle, mid-market, and the luxury *events/bridal* niche — where this range is actively booked.
